## Image Restoration — Disambiguation Overview

This is a disambiguation page, not a substantive article. It identifies that "image restoration" is an umbrella term covering several distinct subfields, ranging from physical photograph conservation to AI-driven digital techniques.

## Key Concepts

- **Image restoration** is not a single technique — it spans physical, digital, and computational methods.
- The term covers at least four distinct areas:
  - **Conservation and restoration of photographs** — physical/chemical preservation of analog photos
  - **Digital photograph restoration** — manual or semi-automated repair of digitized images
  - **Image restoration by artificial intelligence** — learned models that recover degraded images
  - **Iterative reconstruction** — algorithmic approaches (commonly used in medical imaging, e.g., CT/MRI)
- **Image restoration theory** is listed separately, suggesting a formal mathematical framework underpins the applied methods.

## Relationships

- **Image restoration theory** provides the mathematical foundation (inverse problems, PSF estimation, regularization) for the applied subfields.
- **AI-based restoration** builds on classical restoration theory but replaces hand-crafted priors with learned ones (deep learning).
- **Iterative reconstruction** overlaps with medical imaging (CT, MRI, PET) and computational photography.
- **Physical conservation** is distinct from the digital/computational branches — it involves chemical treatments, environmental controls, and archival science.

## Exam-Relevant Points

- Know that "image restoration" is an overloaded term — an exam question may require distinguishing physical conservation from computational restoration.
- Iterative reconstruction is the bridge between image restoration and medical/scientific imaging domains.
- AI-based restoration is a distinct subfield from classical digital restoration — the distinction matters for understanding the evolution of techniques.
- Image restoration theory (inverse problems, degradation models) is the theoretical backbone shared across the computational approaches.
